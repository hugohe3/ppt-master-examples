The takeaway is that Paper2Assets reads the PDF once and produces one bundle that every generator consumes verbatim, so the expensive figure cleanup runs once instead of once per artifact. The chevrons follow Section 2.1: pull the text and captions and crop each figure with a column-aware margin; synthesize the metadata from the first page and, for arXiv papers, the abstract page; write the nine-section summary — Problem, Motivation, Contribution, Method, Dataset or Benchmark, Key Result, Ablation, Headline Numbers, Takeaway — each with an essential entry, a supplementary entry held in reserve, and a spoken script; fetch institution logos from Wikimedia Commons infoboxes and the venue logo from Wikidata, hiding the slot rather than showing a placeholder when a mark does not resolve; render the QR codes; and emit the canonical record files. A second pass adds the inventory manifest with the PDF checksum, per-section records with stable identifiers, and the narration clip list in reading order. The cleanup chain below is the load-bearing stage: a deterministic prefix handles the easy cases, a visual-AI step judges a tight bounding box, a fresh-context verifier re-reads the original against the proposed crop, and only a clean pass commits, splitting a raster that packs two figures. The panel on the right states the single-owner rule: downstream skills never re-open the PDF, and a stable figure-naming invariant keeps the poster, video, and blog pointing at the same figure without coordination.
