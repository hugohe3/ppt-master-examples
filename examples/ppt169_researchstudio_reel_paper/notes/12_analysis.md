The takeaway is a tension the authors state plainly: the PaperQuiz ordering is close to the reverse of the aesthetic ordering. The scatter plots aesthetic overall against PaperQuiz detail accuracy for the nine systems. P2P leads both quiz splits because its portrait canvas is packed with near-verbatim prose, at some of the lowest aesthetic scores; the Paper2Poster tool is close behind because its content is assembled from the benchmark's own question-and-answer extraction; the author posters prune to headline results and place last on both quiz splits; the single-shot models are strong on content and intermediate on aesthetics; ResearchStudio-Reel sits deliberately between the poles because the fill loop packs each column to a target density rather than to exhaustion. The bars on the right are the two controlled comparisons already present in Table 1. First, the same model with and without the loop: claude-opus-4.8 prompted once reaches 2.92 aesthetic and 2.97 Layout; wrapped in the composition step and the measured-fill loop it reaches 3.56 and 3.99 — a gain of 0.64 and more than a full Layout point, although the comparison also changes prompting, the number of calls, and the inference budget. Second, the same skill under Codex with gpt-5.5 reaches 3.27, the second-highest aesthetic score in the table, ahead of every single-shot baseline, every reproduced pipeline, and the author reference. The caution box keeps the authors' own words: the sub-criteria are scored by VLM judges that stand in for but do not equal human preference, so a higher score means the raters prefer the poster, not that human viewers would.
