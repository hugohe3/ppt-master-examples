The takeaway is that three application contexts drove the design and deliberately span the dissemination spectrum. Section 4.1, the author's camera-ready last mile: one Claude Code session per paper produces the editable poster, the video deck and MP4 pair, and the bilingual Word pair, navigable through one reel.html that doubles as a review surface for co-authors. Section 4.2, lab- and organization-level communication: the pipeline is wired into publication intake so every accepted paper yields draft artifacts a communications team polishes; the editable outputs make it a draft-and-revise workflow. Section 4.3, educational reuse: a course or reading group turns a reading list into paper-of-the-week briefing packs and re-runs on a corrected PDF without manual re-extraction. The audits at the bottom are Tables 2 and 3: they count which objects a video or blog system emits — visual cues, editable PPTX, duration control, narration audio; layout checks, embedded figures, editable DOCX, paper-input summarization — and document breadth of the delivery contract without measuring editing effort or the viewer's usability. The gap-to-human panel is the paper's own: a human designer adds diagrams and icons the paper does not contain, and the pipeline reuses only what appears in the paper. Next, limitations.
