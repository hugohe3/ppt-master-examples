# 01_cover

This is a paper-reading walkthrough of ResearchStudio-Reel, arXiv 2607.04438, version two of 19 July 2026, by Hugo He, Lingao Xiao, Yalun Dai, Yangyu Huang and sixteen co-authors across Microsoft Research and seven universities. The hook is on the slide: one paper PDF goes in, three editor-ready artifacts come out — a conference poster, a talk video, and a bilingual blog — and one navigable surface binds them. The three panels are Figure 1 of the paper: a generated poster for the BEiT paper, a frame from the generated talk video for the CITransNet paper, and the English Word article for BEiT. We will follow the paper in its own order: the question, prior work, the contribution, each of the five skills, the benchmark result and its analysis, cost, applications, limitations, and what the authors leave open.

---

# 02_question

The takeaway of this page is that dissemination is a separate layer with three concrete gaps. The paper's introduction describes the layer as three artifacts prepared in the days right after acceptance — the poster, the talk video on the virtual track or the lab channel, and the blog piece for social media — each needing figure selection, layout, narration, or audience-appropriate writing. The literature on paper-to-poster, paper-to-video, and paper-to-blog systems, together with the multi-artifact suites PaperX and OmniPresent, has moved the practical question from whether several formats can be generated to whether the results remain native-editable and navigable. The three gaps are the paper's own labels. G1, shared grounding: combining artifact-specific systems repeats extraction and leaves cross-artifact references to the user. G2, native editability: a multi-format suite does not by itself give editable PowerPoint and Word sources. G3, experience-level convergence: parallel outputs do not bind poster regions, video segments, and blog passages into one surface. Section 1. Next, where the paper places itself among prior systems.

# 03_prior_work

The takeaway is that prior work is organized by output type and differs in its output contract, while VLM scores are mostly used for evaluation rather than as runtime release gates. The table condenses Section 5. Paper-to-poster systems include Paper2Poster with its benchmark and PaperQuiz probe, PosterGen with specialized agents including color and typography stages, P2P which renders HTML, PosterForest, EfficientPosterGen, APEX which edits existing PPTX, the SciPostLayout and SciPostGen layout work, Any2Poster, and the concurrent PosterHarness. Slide and video work runs from PPSGen through D2S, DOC2PPT, SlideSpawn, PPTAgent, and SlideGen to PresentAgent, VideoAgent, Paper2Video with its PaperTalker system and 101-paper benchmark, Preacher, and AutoLectures. Blog and long-form work includes HERA, PTSPI, GoSum, LongDPO, ProjectMundo, lay-summary studies, and Papers-to-Posts. The multi-artifact suites are PaperX and the concurrent OmniPresent, which the paper discusses qualitatively rather than as a baseline. The positioning sentence at the bottom is quoted from Section 5.5: the paper does not claim the first multi-artifact generator; it claims native editability plus experience-level convergence, backed by release checks. # 04_contribution

The takeaway is the answer to the three gaps: a native-editable dissemination workspace implemented as five skills that run in Claude Code and Codex. Figure 2 shows the pipeline — one PDF in, three editor-ready artifacts out, one shared extraction in the middle. The list on the right names the five skills in pipeline order: Paper2Assets, the shared extractor, answers G1; Paper2Poster, Paper2Video, and Paper2Blog are the editable generators whose PowerPoint and Word deliverables answer G2; Paper2Reel, the convergence layer, answers G3. The four contribution bullets are the authors' own, quoted from the end of Section 1, including the headline numbers we will see in Table 1: best among automated systems on all three aesthetic sub-criteria, best or tied-best on two of three information sub-criteria, about seventeen and a half percent above the authors' own posters on aesthetics — 3.56 against 3.03 — and overall wins on 74 and 95 of 100 papers under the two judges. The note at the bottom lists the technical ingredients: the two skill runtimes, deterministic primitives such as headless Chromium, LibreOffice with ffmpeg, and python-docx, and Edge TTS for narration; the paper states that the runtime itself is not a contribution. Next, skill one.

# 05_paper2assets

Skill one of five. The takeaway is that Paper2Assets reads the PDF once and produces one bundle that every generator consumes verbatim, so the expensive figure cleanup runs once instead of once per artifact. The chevrons follow Section 2.1: pull the text and captions and crop each figure with a column-aware margin; synthesize the metadata from the first page and, for arXiv papers, the abstract page; write the nine-section summary — Problem, Motivation, Contribution, Method, Dataset or Benchmark, Key Result, Ablation, Headline Numbers, Takeaway — each with an essential entry, a supplementary entry held in reserve, and a spoken script; fetch institution logos from Wikimedia Commons infoboxes and the venue logo from Wikidata, hiding the slot rather than showing a placeholder when a mark does not resolve; render the QR codes; and emit the canonical record files. A second pass adds the inventory manifest with the PDF checksum, per-section records with stable identifiers, and the narration clip list in reading order. The cleanup chain below is the load-bearing stage: a deterministic prefix handles the easy cases, a visual-AI step judges a tight bounding box, a fresh-context verifier re-reads the original against the proposed crop, and only a clean pass commits, splitting a raster that packs two figures. The panel on the right states the single-owner rule: downstream skills never re-open the PDF, and a stable figure-naming invariant keeps the poster, video, and blog pointing at the same figure without coordination. Next, skill two.

# 06_paper2poster

Skill two of five. The takeaway is that the engineering that took the most iteration was not the visual design but five recurring requirements, labeled A1 to A5 in Section 2.2.1, and the page answers them in the paper's order. Figure 3 shows the pipeline: the bundle drives an agent that picks the Method figure plus secondary figures, composes a self-contained poster.html along four axes, runs the staged-fill loop, packs narration audio and logos, renders PDF and PNG plus an editable native-shape PowerPoint, and releases only through a mandatory deliverables gate. The list on the right gives the four coupled formats of one run. The table: A1, composition without a template explosion, is answered by four orthogonal axes — layout, style with eleven interchangeable CSS themes, five header arrangements, and the Scan-to-Read block — where only the layout is fixed by a hard rule and every section carries a distinct widget. A2, the converging fill loop, gets its own page next. A3, a page too large to re-read, is answered by returning only the verbatim source of off-target sections so the hundred-kilobyte file never enters the context window. A4, figures that fill their cards, is answered by sizing figures through their height cap and holding them to a hard floor. A5, a faithful editable export, is answered by rebuilding each slide from the live DOM into native text frames, replaceable pictures, native equations, and styled shapes. Next, the loop itself.

# 07_fill_loop

Still skill two. The takeaway is that the fill loop is deliberately discrete and terminates at a categorical fixed point rather than a learned-score plateau. The bar is the fullRatio axis — painted content height divided by the inner card height, read from getBoundingClientRect — quantized into the five verdicts of Section 2.2.2: EMPTY below 0.70, SPARSE from 0.70 to 0.90, FULL from 0.90 to 0.98 as the target, SPILLAGE from 0.98 to 1.10, and OVERFLOW above 1.10. The verdict alone selects the move, listed beneath: append or promote, polish up, leave untouched, polish down, drop. The Method figure is retuned under a separate figure-fill gate requiring at least ninety percent of its card on one axis. Three mechanisms damp oscillation: moves are sized by the signed pixel delta the measurement reports; the loop refuses to re-apply a move that already overshot on a section; and an on-disk round counter trips a circuit breaker that ships the best-measured state. The gate is deliberately loose at ninety percent because tightening toward ninety-eight sharply increases rounds past 0.94; a single render-time pass then stretches under-filled cards toward ninety-eight percent by growing row whitespace, without resizing figures. Figure 4 on the right shows the loop on the Latent Diffusion Models poster: the initial exploration, the loop in progress, and the completed poster, with the debug overlay colored by verdict. Next, skill three.

# 08_paper2video

Skill three of five. The takeaway is that a good slide deck does not by itself make a reliable paper video, so Paper2Video focuses on five media-level requirements after the deck leaves the authoring workflow. The deck itself comes from the full ppt-master route, reference 22, which is why the paper treats slide design as a dependency rather than a claim. Figure 5 shows the flow: assets, paced narration, deck authoring, anchored visual cues, render, and the fit gate. The three root deliverables on the right are video.pptx, video.mp4, and video_no_subtitles.mp4, with everything else under assets. The table follows Section 2.3: B1, duration planned before rendering, with semantic rewrites before TTS and a bounded speech-rate plan for small residuals; B2, attention guidance, with semantic anchors, a cue resolver producing visual_cues.json, and the spotlight_laser default; B3, two caption contexts, with burned-in subtitles in one file and a clean source for the reel's own toggle; B4, addressability after export through timeline.json; and B5, deterministic media checks in check_video_package.py, whose list is on the right. Next, skill four.

# 09_paper2blog

Skill four of five. The takeaway is that Paper2Blog is not a bilingual summarizer: it writes the Chinese and English articles separately from one shared evidence map and then gates the Word files on facts, figures, fonts, and page layout. Figure 7 shows the flow from assets to the evidence map, the multilingual drafts, the editorial QA loop, and the two DOCX deliverables — a WeChat public-account piece and a research-blog piece that share facts, figures, numbers, claims, and links but not pacing or examples. The table follows Section 2.4: C1, one evidence base for two languages, where a missing hard fact is omitted rather than guessed; C2, register controlled during writing through language-specific outlines and a style guide, because a deterministic script cannot check register afterwards; C3, a small shared figure set placed next to the section that prepares the reader; C4, a Word article with fixed filenames, embedded media, and a Latin body font plus a Chinese fallback; and C5, layout treated as a rendered artifact, catching underfilled images, images pushed to the next page, orphan tails, and in strict mode near-blank pages. The gate line at the bottom lists the deterministic checks, which compare the two documents on concrete divergences rather than writing style. Next, skill five.

# 10_paper2reel

Skill five of five. The takeaway is that Paper2Reel is a presentation surface rather than a fourth generated summary: one alignment sidecar makes a poster block, a video segment, a slide thumbnail, and a blog passage into different views of the same section. Figure 9 shows the two interaction states from Section 2.5.1: hovering a poster section highlights it while neighbors fade, and double-clicking opens the section modal with video and captions on the left, a language switch in the header, and the aligned blog passage on the right. The viewer is poster-first because the poster is the most compact map of the paper; playback uses the subtitle-free source with captions from sidecars so the toggle never doubles subtitles. The alignment sidecar maps each canonical section id to its poster block, slide targets, video window, subtitle tracks, thumbnails, and blog blocks; the viewer never scrapes filenames or the video. Invoked on a PDF, an arXiv link, or a partial bundle, it completes missing stages through the full upstream workflows so section ids and paths stay consistent. The gates on the right validate the reel as an interactive artifact: a static gate over the bundle, a browser gate that serves the bundle with a range-capable local server and exercises every interaction headlessly, and a file-browser gate for opening from disk. Next, the evidence.

# 11_table1

The takeaway is on the slide: on the 100-paper Paper2Poster benchmark, the Claude Code configuration is the best automated system on all three aesthetic sub-criteria — 3.56 overall against the authors' 3.03 — and best or tied-best on two of three information sub-criteria; on PaperQuiz it ranks below the denser systems. The protocol from Section 3: three families scored with the same pipeline — single-shot Claude-4.8 Opus, GPT-5.5, and Gemini-3.1 Pro with one fixed prompt; the reproduced pipelines Paper2Poster tool, P2P, and PosterGen; ResearchStudio-Reel under Claude Code with claude-opus-4.8 and under Codex with gpt-5.5 — plus the author ground truth. Two VLM judges, claude-opus-4.8 and gpt-5.5, score each poster and each cell is their mean; every poster is downscaled to at most 2560 pixels and rendered at its true content size; PaperQuiz is exact-match answer accuracy from the poster image alone. Read across the Reel Claude Code row: 3.53, 3.17, 3.99, 3.56 on the aesthetic side, 3.99, 4.03, 3.41, 3.81 on the information side, 56.79 and 92.16 on the quiz splits. Bold marks the best value per column among the systems. Below the table: the overall score, the mean of the two Overall columns, puts Claude Code first at 3.69, the single-shot models and the Codex configuration in a second tier, and the reproduced pipelines behind; per paper, Claude Code beats the authors' poster on 74 of 100 papers under the Claude judge and 95 under the GPT judge. # 12_analysis

The takeaway is a tension the authors state plainly: the PaperQuiz ordering is close to the reverse of the aesthetic ordering. The scatter plots aesthetic overall against PaperQuiz detail accuracy for the nine systems. P2P leads both quiz splits because its portrait canvas is packed with near-verbatim prose, at some of the lowest aesthetic scores; the Paper2Poster tool is close behind because its content is assembled from the benchmark's own question-and-answer extraction; the author posters prune to headline results and place last on both quiz splits; the single-shot models are strong on content and intermediate on aesthetics; ResearchStudio-Reel sits deliberately between the poles because the fill loop packs each column to a target density rather than to exhaustion. The bars on the right are the two controlled comparisons already present in Table 1. First, the same model with and without the loop: claude-opus-4.8 prompted once reaches 2.92 aesthetic and 2.97 Layout; wrapped in the composition step and the measured-fill loop it reaches 3.56 and 3.99 — a gain of 0.64 and more than a full Layout point, although the comparison also changes prompting, the number of calls, and the inference budget. Second, the same skill under Codex with gpt-5.5 reaches 3.27, the second-highest aesthetic score in the table, ahead of every single-shot baseline, every reproduced pipeline, and the author reference. The caution box keeps the authors' own words: the sub-criteria are scored by VLM judges that stand in for but do not equal human preference, so a higher score means the raters prefer the poster, not that human viewers would. The takeaway is qualitative: holding the skill, prompt, and pipeline fixed, five configurations each produce a full single page that differs in figure choice, phrasing density, and accent. # 14_operational_profile

The takeaway is that a full four-artifact bundle takes about 89 minutes of summed stage time and about 2.6 million input and 276 thousand output tokens per paper, averaged over five papers on claude-opus-4-8, from Table 4. The bars are the per-skill subtotals: Paper2Assets 8.6 minutes, Paper2Poster 23.3, Paper2Video 28.5, Paper2Blog 16.7, Paper2Reel 12.1. The table names each skill's heaviest stage: extraction and figures for Paper2Assets at eighteen percent of input tokens; the fill loop for Paper2Poster at 14.8 minutes and eighteen percent of output; render and mux for Paper2Video; the QA gate and revision for Paper2Blog at seventeen percent of output; and the QA gate for Paper2Reel. The totals strip gives 675 turns, 2,568 thousand distinct input tokens, 108,546 thousand cached re-reads billed at roughly ten percent of fresh input, and 276 thousand output tokens. The notes below repeat the paper's qualifications: each skill runs as an isolated subprocess whose one-time context load folds into its first stage, running the three generators in parallel reduces elapsed time, and when the LaTeX source is available the pipeline can read original figure files instead of the PDF path. # 15_applications

The takeaway is that three application contexts drove the design and deliberately span the dissemination spectrum. Section 4.1, the author's camera-ready last mile: one Claude Code session per paper produces the editable poster, the video deck and MP4 pair, and the bilingual Word pair, navigable through one reel.html that doubles as a review surface for co-authors. Section 4.2, lab- and organization-level communication: the pipeline is wired into publication intake so every accepted paper yields draft artifacts a communications team polishes; the editable outputs make it a draft-and-revise workflow. Section 4.3, educational reuse: a course or reading group turns a reading list into paper-of-the-week briefing packs and re-runs on a corrected PDF without manual re-extraction. The audits at the bottom are Tables 2 and 3: they count which objects a video or blog system emits — visual cues, editable PPTX, duration control, narration audio; layout checks, embedded figures, editable DOCX, paper-input summarization — and document breadth of the delivery contract without measuring editing effort or the viewer's usability. The gap-to-human panel is the paper's own: a human designer adds diagrams and icons the paper does not contain, and the pipeline reuses only what appears in the paper. Next, limitations.

# 16_limitations

The takeaway is that the paper gives limitations a real page, and so do we. Appendix A lists five recurring failure modes with one-sentence mitigations: L1, figure-cleanup residue, re-cropped by the decaption pass and the fresh-context verifier; L2, fill-loop non-convergence across the ninety-to-ninety-eight band, handled by the round counter, the circuit breaker, and the render-time expand; L3, slide-narration referential drift, rejected by the QA gate through the shared timeline; L4, bilingual blog drift, cross-checked by the evidence map and the DOCX gate; and L5, voice-mismatched narration, addressed by re-running with a different Edge TTS voice. The domain scope is ML, CV, and NLP venues; transfer to biomedicine, physics, or design-heavy fields is untested. Evaluation coverage is the honest limit: quantitative results exist only for the poster under two VLM judges; the video benchmark of Paper2Video is not applied; the audits do not measure human editing effort, fidelity after edits, or whether section-level navigation improves understanding; the Reader-Reconstruction Preference signal is in-loop rather than a headline metric; and a third-party human-rater study is future work. The panel below quotes Appendix B on provenance: preserve which skill, bundle checksum, theme, voice, and model produced an artifact so readers can audit the chain and disclose AI involvement. Next, the conclusion.

# 17_conclusion

The takeaway restates only what the evidence supports and names the two gaps the authors put ahead of broader coverage. Established: five composable skills, one Paper2Assets bundle, a native-editable PowerPoint poster and video deck plus a bilingual Word blog, bound by Paper2Reel; posters best among automated systems on all three aesthetic sub-criteria and best or tied-best on two of three information sub-criteria under two VLM judges, 3.56 against 3.03, with overall wins on 74 and 95 of 100 papers; and a delivery-contract pattern that may extend to other targets with native authoring formats, deterministic renderers, and testable package criteria, with its limits still to be studied. Open, from Section 6: first, evaluation is proxy-bound — the aesthetic rubric and PaperQuiz pull in opposite directions and neither measures whether a reader absorbs the work, so the honest next step is a controlled human reading-and-recall signal; second, the generative gap — the pipeline reuses only figures that exist in the paper and cannot produce the bespoke diagrams designers add, and faithful figure synthesis would reintroduce the hallucination risk the categorical gates suppress. The paper's closing sentence is the frame for the whole talk: keeping generated artifacts editable and connected shifts the last mile from one-way automation toward a workflow authors can inspect, navigate, and revise.
