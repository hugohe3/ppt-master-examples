So where did the concrete come from? From a critic. Reyner Banham, a British architectural historian, used new brutalism to identify both an ethic and an aesthetic style in his nineteen fifty-five essay, naming Hunstanton and the Soho house as the reference by which the new brutalism in architecture may be defined. He also tied the term to two French phrases: art brut, raw art, and béton brut, raw concrete. Eleven years later, in his book The New Brutalism: Ethic or Aesthetic?, he explained why that second phrase stuck. If there is one single verbal formula that has made the concept of brutalism admissible in most of the world's Western languages, he wrote, it is that Le Corbusier himself described that concrete work as béton-brut. That is the whole origin of the association most people now take for granted: the material got attached to the movement through a critic quoting an architect about somebody else's concrete.
