The word arrived before the concrete, and it arrived in Sweden. The architect Hans Asplund coined the term nybrutalism — new brutalism — to describe Villa Göth, a modern brick home in Uppsala, designed in January nineteen fifty by his contemporaries Bengt Edman and Lennart Holm. There is no tower here, no raw concrete slab, no social housing. What the house had was the as-found approach that would later sit at the core of the movement: visible I-beams over the windows, exposed brick inside and out, and poured concrete keeping the tongue-and-groove pattern of the boards that formed it. Nothing was covered up, and nothing was smoothed. It is worth noting that the house outlived most of the towers built in its name — it was listed as historically significant by the Uppsala county administrative board on the third of March, nineteen ninety-five.
