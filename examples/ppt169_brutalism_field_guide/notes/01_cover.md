Concrete, honest, unforgiving. Those three words are not an insult here — they are close to what the movement asked to be judged by. Brutalist architecture is a style that emerged in the nineteen fifties in the United Kingdom, among the reconstruction projects of the post-war era, and it stayed active until the early nineteen eighties, across every continent. The claim printed on the ink block is the source's own definition: minimalist construction showcasing the bare building materials and structural elements over decorative design. Hold on to that sentence, because everything that follows — the arguments for the style and the campaigns to demolish it — comes out of it. The photograph on the right is not a building, it is a wall: board-marked concrete, the surface this guide is really about.
