To feel the scale the movement worked at, take one building. Mihajlo Mitrović designed the Western City Gate in Belgrade in nineteen seventy-seven — better known as the Genex Tower. Thirty-six storeys. One hundred and seventeen metres, or three hundred and eighty-four feet, rising to between one hundred and thirty-five and one hundred and forty metres with the revolving restaurant at the top. It is formed by two towers joined by a two-storey bridge, designed in the brutalist style with elements of structuralism and constructivism, and it is considered a prime representative of the style in Serbia and one of the best built anywhere in the nineteen sixties and seventies. There is one thing this page deliberately does not tell you. The source calls the Genex Tower the second-tallest high-rise in Belgrade, after the Ušće Tower, but it gives no height for the Ušće Tower. So the comparison is printed as no data rather than estimated. That is a rule this guide follows throughout: where the article states no figure, the page says so.
