If the material is honest, the plan should be too. A common theme in brutalist design is the exposure of the building's inner workings — structure, services, and human use — on the exterior, so that the façade reports what is happening behind it. Boston City Hall, designed in nineteen sixty-two, is the clearest case: the strikingly different and projected portions of the building indicate the special nature of the rooms behind those walls, such as the mayor's office or the city council chambers. You can read the plan off the elevation. Hunstanton School did the same thing with services rather than rooms. The water tank, normally a hidden feature, was put in a prominent visible tower, and the water and electric utilities were delivered by readily visible pipes and conduits instead of being buried in the walls. Two different buildings, one principle: put the thing on the outside.
