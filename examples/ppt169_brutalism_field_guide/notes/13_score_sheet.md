Here is the record, and it runs in both directions at once. On the demolished side: the Tricorn Centre by Owen Luder and Rodney Gordon in two thousand and four; the Third Church of Christ, Scientist in Washington in twenty fourteen; John Madin's Birmingham Central Library in twenty sixteen; the Smithsons' own Robin Hood Gardens in twenty seventeen; and the Welbeck Street car park in twenty nineteen. Marcel Breuer's American Press Institute building in Reston is also gone, though the source gives no year, so the cell says no data — as it does for the architects the article never names. On the saved side, over exactly the same decades: Villa Göth listed in nineteen ninety-five; Preston bus station garage listed Grade Two in twenty thirteen after two failed demolition proposals; Dunelm House saved in twenty twenty-one; and the Southbank Centre, including the Queen Elizabeth Hall and the Hayward Gallery, listed in twenty twenty-six after a thirty-five year campaign. The Twentieth Century Society lost the Tricorn and Trinity Square and won Preston and the Southbank. On that last listing its director, Catherine Croft, said the battle has been won and brutalism has finally come of age — while Simon Heffer responded by calling for the centre's demolition. Both reactions are still live.
