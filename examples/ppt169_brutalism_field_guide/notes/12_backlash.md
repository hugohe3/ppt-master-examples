And then the public turned. Simon Jenkins put it most sharply: few styles in history can have been met with so many pleas from its users to see it destroyed. The Economist made the same observation in twenty fourteen, noting that a campaign to demolish a building will usually be directed against a brutalist one. The most concrete evidence is a television programme. In two thousand and five the British series Demolition ran a public vote to choose twelve buildings that ought to be pulled down, and eight of the twelve selected were brutalist. Part of the case against the style is not aesthetic at all, it is chemical. Concrete façades do not age well in damp, cloudy maritime climates like north-western Europe and New England: the surface streaks with water stains, sometimes moss and lichen, and rust from the steel reinforcing bars bleeds through. On top of that, critics object to the cold appearance and an atmosphere they read as totalitarian. The photograph is what that verdict looks like when it is carried out — and the Queen Elizabeth Square flats in Glasgow, built in nineteen sixty-two, were demolished in nineteen ninety-three.
