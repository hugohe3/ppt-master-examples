So how should you look at the next concrete building you walk past? Not with the reflex, in either direction. Brutalism set its own test, and the fairest thing is to use it. Formal legibility of plan. Clear exhibition of structure. Materials valued for their inherent qualities, as found. That is what its architects meant by calling it an ethic and not an aesthetic, and it is the same honesty that made the buildings hated when the concrete streaked and cheap and cold became the only words people had for them. The style has been polarising throughout its history — public campaigns to demolish brutalist buildings are common, and in the United Kingdom some of those same buildings have now been preserved. Both facts are true at once, and both follow from a movement that refused to hide anything.
