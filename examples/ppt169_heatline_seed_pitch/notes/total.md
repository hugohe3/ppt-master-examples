# 02_why_now

Two years ago this market looked like it was failing. In twenty twenty four European heat pump sales fell twenty one per cent, the largest decline ever recorded, with Germany down almost half and France down a quarter, according to the International Energy Agency. Then twenty twenty five turned: European sales rose eleven per cent, the first growth since twenty twenty two, led by Germany at fifty five per cent in the first half, and it was the first year heat pumps outsold gas boilers in Europe. The columns show the European Union's own installation volumes, two point eight million units in twenty twenty two down to two point one one million in twenty twenty four and back up to two point three four million in twenty twenty five. What makes this durable rather than a rebound is the band along the bottom: under the recast buildings directive, member states stopped funding new stand-alone fossil boilers from the start of twenty twenty five, and national plans have to reach a complete phase-out by twenty forty. The alternative is being removed by law.

# 03_problem

So if demand has a legal floor under it, what is the constraint? It is people. The European Commission's own estimate is that Europe needs seven hundred and fifty thousand more installers, and that at least half of today's installers have to be reskilled to work with heat pumps. Against that, the European heat pump industry employed about a hundred and seventeen thousand people, and only around two thirds of those are outside manufacturing, while the trade body puts the requirement at half a million skilled full-time employees by twenty thirty. In the United Kingdom the ratio is starker still: roughly three thousand heat pump engineers today against at least twenty seven thousand needed by twenty twenty eight. The curve here is a schematic, not a measured series, and it is labelled that way on the page. The evidence that matters most to us is the last line: when Nesta surveyed three hundred and forty five installers, thirty per cent said finding suitable additional staff was the biggest barrier to doing more installations, rising to forty one per cent among firms with six or more staff. I want to be honest about the counter-reading, which is also on the page. Across the whole trade, lack of customer demand was cited more often. Staffing is what binds the firms that already have work, and those are precisely the firms we sell to.

---

# 04_solution

You cannot hire your way out of a shortage like that in one season, so the lever has to be throughput per engineer. That is what Heatline does, and it does three things. It forecasts crew capacity months ahead against accreditation, so you know in January what August looks like. It sequences the survey, install and commission chain against part lead times, so a booking is not confirmed against a unit that has not arrived. And it holds the grant and compliance paperwork on the same job record, so the administrative tail does not eat the engineer's week. The curve you saw a moment ago is the same curve, and it bends because each engineer completes more jobs, not because more engineers appear. It is worth saying what we are not: we do not train engineers and we do not sell equipment. Everything I describe about the product here is illustrative.

# 09_competition

I want to be fair about the alternatives, because two of them are genuinely good at what they do. Shared spreadsheets and calendars are free, instantly flexible and need no implementation, and for a two-engineer business they are often the right answer. Generic field-service software has mature dispatch, invoicing and integrations, and it will beat us on the day of the job for years. What neither of them does is model crew capacity months ahead, or model which engineer is accredited to close which class of job, and that is the whole column on the right. Our capability and price on this page are illustrative and no vendor is named; the competitor pricing is published list pricing. The honest closing line is the one at the bottom: we are not claiming these tools are bad. We are claiming that none of them answers the question an installer asks in January about August.

# 10_go_to_market

There are three channels and only two of them are proven, which the page shows deliberately. Direct sales to firms with six or more engineers proved the product, at a nine week median cycle and a blended acquisition cost of six thousand four hundred pounds, and produced the first twenty four customers. The manufacturer training academies are the repeatable motion: they refer installers at the moment they are newly accredited and actually need planning, at an acquisition cost of two thousand nine hundred pounds, and that channel produced seventeen customers and the September inflection you saw on the traction curve. The third channel, trade-body accreditation partners, is planned for twenty twenty seven, has not been tested, and we are claiming no acquisition cost for it. The reason we think the timing works is real: eighty one per cent of surveyed firms with six or more staff expected to hire within twelve months, and hiring is the moment planning capacity stops being optional. All the channel counts and costs are illustrative.
