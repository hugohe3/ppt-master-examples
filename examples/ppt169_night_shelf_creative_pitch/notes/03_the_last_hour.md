So where is the room? There is exactly one place left, and something else is already sitting in it. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine surveyed two thousand US adults last June: half of them use a screen in bed every single day, another third most days of the week, and a quarter openly say they choose the phone over the sleep they know they need. And that hour has a measurable price. A Norwegian study of forty-five thousand students, published in Frontiers in Psychiatry in March, found that each additional hour of screen time after getting into bed raised the odds of insomnia symptoms by fifty-nine percent and cost twenty-four minutes of sleep. Now here is the part that turns a problem into a campaign. Reading in bed is the one behaviour that has been tested the other way. In a randomised trial of nine hundred and ninety-one people, forty-two percent of those who read a book in bed said their sleep improved, against twenty-eight percent who did not. The bookshop is not selling a nicer habit. It is selling a better one, and it can say so with a straight face.
