Put on one basis, most of the precedents the committee will hear quoted are about a different intervention or a different sector. Iceland moved public-sector workers from forty hours to thirty-five or thirty-six, and Anthony Veal records that in sixty-one of sixty-six workplaces the reduction was only one to three hours, with no monitored control. Belgium's Labour Deal is a right to compress thirty-eight hours into four days at unchanged total hours and pay, which is not 100-80-100 at all. Portugal has the strongest design, including a control group of interested non-adopters, but its own authors call it a proof of concept and say its participants are not representative. Germany has the strongest method but many participants simply cut hours across five days. South Cambridgeshire is a council delivering statutory services. That leaves the UK cohort and its twelve-month follow-up as the only evidence about firms like ours — and the two conflicts at the foot of the page are shown, not reconciled.
