Six risks, and the matrix is arranged so the committee can see that only one of them sits in the top-right cell. Client and stakeholder expectations is that risk, and it is the one preparation cannot remove, because it depends on how clients react rather than on how well we plan; it is also precisely what stopped the only comparable firm in the UK cohort, and what deterred participation in Belgium's reduced-hours pilot. The rest are preparation problems with owners against them: hours creep, which we control by measuring actual hours rather than the policy; the absence of an agreed productivity definition, fixed in phase zero; benefit fade after month three; a dispute when the policy is withdrawn, answered by stating its non-contractual status in writing; and inequitable application, answered by taking whole practice groups rather than individual opt-in. One honest caveat: the risks are sourced, but the likelihood and impact ratings are our judgement, not findings.
