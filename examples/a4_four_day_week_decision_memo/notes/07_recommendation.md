The recommendation is deliberately narrow: two whole practice groups, about sixty people, on full pay with a universal day off and a target thirty-two-hour week, after ten weeks of redesign, running for six months with one interim gate and no change to contracts. Each design choice traces back to the evidence. Redesign comes first because reversion tracks redesign effort and two to three months of preparation is the consistent figure across the UK, Portuguese and six-country studies. A universal day off is chosen because universal and staggered models converted to permanence in 95 percent of cases against 60 percent for flexible ones. Metrics are agreed before the start because half the Portuguese companies could not define productivity metrics at all. And the pilot is stated in writing as non-contractual, because nearly half the permanent cases elsewhere never changed contracts. Three things are explicitly not recommended: a firm-wide commitment, a compressed week, and an hours cut spread across five days.
