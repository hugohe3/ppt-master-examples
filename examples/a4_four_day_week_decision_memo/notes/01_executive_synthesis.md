The governing question is whether this firm should move to a four-day week on the 100-80-100 basis, and the answer this memo defends is a bounded pilot in two practice groups rather than a firm-wide commitment. Three things make that answer credible. The wellbeing effect is large and it survives control groups: burnout fell from 2.8 to 2.34 and stress from 3.07 to 2.74 across the UK cohort, and a controlled study of nearly 2,900 workers in 141 organisations across six countries found the same gains absent in its controls. The business case is a different matter: revenue rose 1.4 percent and resignations fell from 2.0 to 0.8 per hundred employees, but the pilot's own report states those trends cannot be called statistically significant. And professional services is where the model has been observed to fail. The gap to admit, if asked, is scale: 37 percent of the UK cohort had ten staff or fewer, and only a tenth were above a hundred people.
