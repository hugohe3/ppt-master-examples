Persistence is the most-quoted evidence for the four-day week, and it holds up better than the business metrics, provided each rate is read with its base. Fifty-six of the sixty-one UK companies, or 92 percent, were still running the policy at the end of the trial, and at twelve months at least fifty-four, or 89 percent, still were. In Germany, 73 percent were still on reduced hours after the pilot, while a fifth returned to a five-day forty-hour week and two large organisations dropped out early citing economic reasons. The 100 percent figure from the 2024 to 2025 UK pilot rests on seventeen self-selected companies, which is a weaker signal than 89 percent on sixty-one, and this memo deliberately does not lead with it. Two qualifications matter for us. Nearly half the permanent cases never changed contracts, so the entitlement can be withdrawn — and universal or staggered day-off models converted to permanence far more often than flexible ones.
