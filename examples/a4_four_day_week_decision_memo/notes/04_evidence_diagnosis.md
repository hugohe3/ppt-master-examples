This is the page to hold on to when someone quotes a headline. What the evidence establishes is worker wellbeing: the six-country controlled study found gains in burnout, job satisfaction and mental and physical health that its control organisations did not show, and the German pilot, instrumented with within-organisation controls, smartwatches and cortisol samples, found the same falls in stress and burnout. What it does not establish is the business case. The UK 2022 report states in its own words that its resignation, new-hire and absence trends cannot be called statistically significant, given small samples and labour-market contingencies, and Germany found sick days, turnover and profit changed only slightly and not significantly. That is why the two grey bars on the chart carry that label at the same size as the number. The methodological pattern behind all of it — self-selection, self-reported financials, absent or small control arms, shrinking follow-up samples — is documented in the primary reports themselves, not asserted by critics.
