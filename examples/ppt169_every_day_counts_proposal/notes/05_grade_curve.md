Across the K to twelve span in twenty twenty two twenty three, the rate is worst at both ends: forty five point six percent in kindergarten and forty five point two percent in grade twelve, with the trough at grade five still near twenty nine percent. The kindergarten peak is the one that usually surprises a board, and it is the one with the longest consequences. The Framework for Action reports that students chronically absent in both kindergarten and first grade are much less likely to read at grade level by third grade, and that students not reading at grade level by third grade are four times more likely to drop out. By sixth grade chronic absenteeism is itself a leading dropout indicator, and by high school attendance predicts dropout better than test scores do. So a system that only watches high school is watching one end of a two ended problem.
