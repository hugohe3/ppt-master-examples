The tiers only mean something if each one has a trigger, an owner and a limit, so here they are. Tier one triggers on enrolment: attendance expectations, positive messaging and daily attendance data in front of every teacher, owned by the site principal. Tier two triggers when a student passes the early warning threshold in a rolling window: same week family contact, a named adult, and a barrier check covering health and transport, owned by the site attendance lead. Tier three triggers when a student is already chronically absent or is referred by the team: case review, a home visit, a community partner referral and an attendance plan agreed with the family, owned by the district attendance coordinator. The thresholds and role titles here are illustrative. The limit is the part I want to be explicit about. The Framework treats legal action as a very last resort, and England's statutory guidance takes the same shape, with legal intervention reserved for cases where attendance support does not work, is not engaged with, or is not appropriate. This system escalates on data, not on frustration.
